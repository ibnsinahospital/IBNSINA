High Blood Pressure in Jammu & Kashmir: Symptoms, Causes, Risks & When to See a Doctor

📅 03 September 2026 ⏱ 3 min read Ibn Sina Hospital
High blood pressure, also called hypertension, is one of the most common health conditions across Jammu & Kashmir, and also one of the easiest to miss. It rarely announces itself with dramatic symptoms, which is exactly why so many people live with it for years without knowing. Here's what's genuinely useful to understand. Why It's Often Called the "Silent" Condition Most people with high blood pressure feel completely normal. There's no obvious warning sign telling you something is wrong, which is part of what makes it dangerous. By the time symptoms do appear, blood pressure has often been elevated for a long time, quietly putting strain on the heart, kidneys, and blood vessels. Symptoms to Watch For While high blood pressure is often silent, some people do notice signs, especially when levels are very high. These can include frequent headaches, particularly at the back of the head, dizziness or lightheadedness, blurred vision, shortness of breath, nosebleeds, or a feeling of pounding in the chest or ears. None of these symptoms are exclusive to high blood pressure, which is exactly why a proper check is the only reliable way to know. What Causes It A combination of factors typically contributes to high blood pressure. Diet plays a significant role, particularly high salt intake, which is common in much of Kashmiri and Jammu cuisine. Limited physical activity, stress, smoking, excess weight, and family history all add to the risk. Colder months can also cause blood vessels to constrict, which is part of why blood pressure readings sometimes trend higher during winter across the region. The Risks of Leaving It Unchecked Untreated high blood pressure doesn't just raise a number, it steadily increases the risk of heart attack, stroke, kidney damage, and vision problems. Because it develops gradually, many people don't realize the toll it's taking until a more serious event forces the issue. This is exactly why proactive checkups matter more than waiting for a symptom to demand attention. Who Should Be Getting Checked Regularly Anyone over 40 should have their blood pressure checked at least once a year, even without symptoms. If you have diabetes, a family history of heart disease, are overweight, or lead a largely sedentary lifestyle, more frequent monitoring is worth discussing with your doctor. Pregnant women should also have blood pressure monitored closely, as it can affect both mother and baby. When to See a Doctor If you've never had your blood pressure checked, that alone is reason enough to book an appointment. If you already know you have high blood pressure but haven't had it reviewed in a while, or your readings have been inconsistent, it's worth getting reassessed rather than assuming things are stable. And if you experience sudden, severe headache, chest pain, confusion, or difficulty speaking, alongside known high blood pressure, this can signal a hypertensive emergency and needs immediate medical attention, not a scheduled appointment. How We Can Help At Ibn Sina Hospital, we offer blood pressure evaluation as part of routine consultations, along with ABPM, ambulatory blood pressure monitoring, which tracks your blood pressure over a full 24-hour period rather than relying on a single reading. This gives a far more accurate picture, especially for patients whose blood pressure fluctuates throughout the day. Our consultant-led team can help interpret results and guide next steps, whether that's lifestyle changes, medication, or further testing.
