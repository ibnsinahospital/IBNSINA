Kangri Cancer: The Winter Cancer No One Talks About in Kashmir

Kangri cancer is a unique form of skin cancer found almost only in Kashmir. Learn the warning signs, who's at risk, and how to prevent it. Expert advice from Ibn Sina Hospital, Budgam.

📅 19 September 2026 • ⏱ 7 min read • Ibn Sina Hospital
Share

Every winter, families across the Kashmir Valley bring out the Kangri. The earthen pot wrapped in wickerwork, filled with glowing charcoal, held beneath the Pheran — it is warmth, comfort, and tradition all in one. For generations, it has kept Kashmiris alive through Chilai Kalan, the harshest forty days of winter.

But there is something most Kashmiri families have never been told.

Holding the Kangri against your skin for years can cause cancer. It is called Kangri cancer, and it is found almost nowhere else in the world except the Kashmir Valley.

At Ibn Sina Hospital in Ompora, Budgam, our doctors want every family to know about this silent danger — because it is preventable, and when caught early, it is treatable.

What Is Kangri Cancer?

Kangri cancer is a type of skin cancer that develops from years of holding the Kangri against bare skin — usually on the inner thighs or abdomen.

The constant heat — around 45 to 50 degrees Celsius — slowly damages the skin over many winters. At first, the skin becomes discoloured and dark. Over time, it can turn into a serious, aggressive cancer called squamous cell carcinoma.

This cancer has been documented in medical books for over 100 years. But even today, most Kashmiri families have never heard of it.

Who Is at Risk?

You may be at risk if you:

  • Have been using a Kangri every winter for many years — especially 10+ winters
  • Hold the Kangri directly against your skin under the Pheran
  • Have noticed dark, thickened, or scarred skin on your thighs or abdomen
  • Are above 45 years of age and have used a Kangri most winters
  • Have a history of other skin cancers

Both men and women are affected. In fact, the same type of cancer used to occur in Japan (called Kairo cancer) from similar heating pots — but it has largely disappeared there because of safer heating options.

Early Warning Signs to Watch For

Kangri cancer develops slowly. Watch for these signs on your inner thighs, abdomen, or wherever you hold the Kangri:

Early Signs (Reversible)

  • Dark, mottled, or pigmented patches on the skin
  • Skin that feels thickened or leathery
  • Mild redness or discolouration
  • Itching or slight burning

These are signs of erythema ab igne — the medical name for skin damage from chronic heat exposure. If you see this, act now.

Advanced Warning Signs (Urgent)

  • A rough or scaly patch that won't heal
  • A sore or ulcer that bleeds or crusts
  • A growing lump or hard nodule
  • A wound that hasn't healed in 4+ weeks
  • Bleeding from a formerly dark patch

If you notice any of these, see a doctor immediately.

Why This Cancer Is So Dangerous

Kangri cancer is not like most skin cancers. It is aggressive. If it is ignored, it can:

  • Grow deeper into underlying tissue
  • Spread to muscles and bones
  • Reach the lymph nodes
  • In rare cases, invade the brain (especially when it appears on the scalp)

Once the cancer has spread, treatment becomes far more difficult and the prognosis worsens. This is why early detection is everything.

How to Prevent Kangri Cancer

Prevention is simple — and it starts this winter.

1. Never Put the Kangri Against Bare Skin

Always place a thick cloth, towel, or blanket between the Kangri and your skin. This simple layer reduces the heat reaching your skin by a large amount.

2. Limit Continuous Use

Don't hold the Kangri in one position for hours. Move it around. Take breaks.

3. Check Your Skin Every Winter

Look at your thighs and abdomen once a month during winter. If you notice new dark patches, thickened skin, or any sore that won't heal, see a doctor.

4. Consider Safer Heating Options

Where possible, use modern safe alternatives:

  • Electric heaters (with proper ventilation)
  • Hot water bags wrapped in cloth
  • Properly installed Bukharis (with ventilation)
  • Heated blankets

5. Do Not Ignore Long-Standing Skin Changes

Many patients come to us years after the first skin change — because they thought it was just "Kangri marks." It isn't. Get it checked.

How Is Kangri Cancer Diagnosed and Treated?

At Ibn Sina Hospital, we offer:

  • ✅ Skin examination by experienced doctors
  • ✅ Skin biopsy to confirm the diagnosis
  • ✅ Surgical removal of early lesions
  • ✅ Referral for advanced oncology care when needed
  • ✅ Regular follow-up for patients with previous Kangri damage

When caught early, Kangri cancer is treatable and often curable with surgery alone. When caught late, it becomes much more serious.

Why This Matters for Every Kashmiri Family

The Kangri is not the enemy. It is part of our culture, our history, and our survival through harsh winters. But we need to use it safely.

If one person in every Kashmiri household learns to place a cloth between the Kangri and their skin, we could reduce Kangri cancer cases dramatically within one generation.

This is the kind of change that begins with a conversation — in your home, with your parents and grandparents.

Do not wait for the sore to get worse. Do not assume it is "just the Kangri."

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Kangri cancer?

Kangri cancer is a type of skin cancer (squamous cell carcinoma) that develops from years of holding the Kangri against bare skin. It is found almost exclusively in the Kashmir Valley.

Is Kangri cancer common in Kashmir?

Yes. It is one of the most common skin cancers in Kashmir, especially in older adults who have used a Kangri for many years.

Can Kangri cancer be prevented?

Yes. Always place a cloth between the Kangri and your skin. Limit continuous use. Check your skin regularly. Use safer heating options when possible.

Is Kangri cancer curable?

When caught early, Kangri cancer is often curable with surgery alone. When diagnosed late, it becomes much more aggressive and difficult to treat.

How long does it take for Kangri cancer to develop?

Usually 10 to 30 years of regular Kangri use. The longer the exposure, the higher the risk.

What does early Kangri skin damage look like?

Dark, mottled, or thickened patches on the inner thighs or abdomen. This is called erythema ab igne. If you see it, act now.

Does Kangri cancer only affect older people?

It is most common in people above 45, but cases have been reported in younger adults who have used a Kangri regularly for many years.

What is the treatment for Kangri cancer?

Early-stage lesions are treated with surgical removal. Advanced cases may need radiation or chemotherapy. Early detection greatly improves outcomes.

Can a cloth really reduce the risk?

Yes. A thick cloth reduces the heat reaching the skin significantly. This simple layer can prevent the chronic skin damage that leads to cancer.
