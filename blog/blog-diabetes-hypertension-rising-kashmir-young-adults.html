Why Diabetes and Hypertension Are Rising Among Kashmir's Young Adults

2026-09-14

Diabetes and high blood pressure (hypertension) are no longer "old people's diseases." In Jammu & Kashmir, we are now seeing these conditions in people as young as 25, 30, and 35 — many of whom don't realise they're at risk until a serious health problem appears.

At Ibn Sina Hospital in Ompora, Budgam, our general medicine, cardiology, and nephrology teams are seeing this trend firsthand. Young patients from Budgam, Srinagar, Ganderbal, Pulwama, and across Kashmir are coming in with elevated blood sugar, high blood pressure, and early signs of heart and kidney damage.

This guide explains why this is happening and what you can do to protect yourself and your family.

Why Are Young Kashmiris Getting Diabetes and High BP?

Several factors are combining to drive this rise:

1. Changing Diet

Traditional Kashmiri food was largely seasonal, locally sourced, and moderate in portion size. Today's diet includes:

  • Refined carbohydrates (white rice, bakery items, sweets)
  • Processed and packaged foods
  • Sugary drinks and tea with extra sugar
  • High intake of red meat and ghee
  • Fewer fresh vegetables and fruits in daily meals

This dietary shift has increased obesity and insulin resistance — the two biggest drivers of Type 2 diabetes.

2. Sedentary Lifestyle

Less physical activity due to:

  • Long hours sitting — at work or on mobile phones
  • Lack of regular walking or exercise
  • Reduced outdoor activity during long winter months

3. Rising Stress Levels

Chronic stress raises blood pressure and cortisol — both directly linked to diabetes and hypertension. Young adults face:

  • Job and financial pressure
  • Uncertainty and anxiety
  • Poor sleep quality

4. Family History and Genetics

South Asian populations — including Kashmiris — have a genetic predisposition to insulin resistance and heart disease. If your parents or grandparents have diabetes or high BP, your risk is higher.

5. Delayed Diagnosis

Because early diabetes and hypertension have almost no symptoms, many young adults don't get tested until damage has already started.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

Warning Signs of Diabetes

  • Constant thirst
  • Frequent urination (especially at night)
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Constant fatigue
  • Blurred vision
  • Slow-healing wounds
  • Dark patches on neck or armpits

Warning Signs of High Blood Pressure

  • Frequent headaches (especially in the morning)
  • Dizziness or lightheadedness
  • Shortness of breath
  • Nosebleeds (unexplained)
  • Chest discomfort
  • Vision problems
  • Nose or ear ringing

Important: In many cases, there are no symptoms at all. This is why hypertension is called the "silent killer." The only way to know is to get tested.

Who Should Get Screened?

You should get your blood sugar and blood pressure checked if you:

  • Are over 25 and haven't been tested in the last 2 years
  • Have a parent or sibling with diabetes or hypertension
  • Are overweight (especially around the abdomen)
  • Have a sedentary job or lifestyle
  • Eat a high-carb or high-fat diet
  • Feel constantly tired or unwell
  • Have PCOS (women)
  • Had gestational diabetes during pregnancy

What You Can Do

1. Get Tested Early

A simple blood test (fasting sugar, HbA1c) and a blood pressure check can catch problems before they become serious. At Ibn Sina Hospital, we offer these tests 24/7 with rapid results.

2. Change Your Plate

  • Reduce white rice — choose brown rice or millet
  • Cut down on bakery items and sweets
  • Add more vegetables and fresh fruit
  • Limit red meat and ghee
  • Reduce salt to less than 5g per day

3. Move More

  • Walk 30 minutes daily
  • Take stairs instead of lifts
  • Do light exercise at home — even 15 minutes helps
  • During winter, exercise indoors

4. Manage Stress

  • Sleep 7–8 hours daily
  • Practice deep breathing or prayer
  • Take breaks from mobile and screens
  • Talk to family and friends

5. Stop Smoking and Limit Alcohol

Both directly raise blood pressure and increase diabetes risk. Quitting is one of the best decisions you can make.

When to See a Doctor

Visit a doctor immediately if you have:

  • Blood sugar above 200 mg/dL (random)
  • Blood pressure above 140/90 mmHg
  • Chest pain, shortness of breath, or dizziness
  • Sudden vision changes
  • Severe headaches with confusion
  • Unexplained weight loss

Frequently Asked Questions

Can young people get diabetes?

Yes. Type 2 diabetes is now commonly diagnosed in people in their 20s and 30s — especially those with a family history, poor diet, or sedentary lifestyle.

Is high blood pressure dangerous in young adults?

Yes. Untreated hypertension can silently damage your heart, kidneys, brain, and eyes over time. Early detection and treatment prevent this damage.

Can diabetes and hypertension be reversed?

Early-stage Type 2 diabetes and hypertension can often be controlled or reversed with weight loss, diet, and exercise. Advanced cases require medication.

How often should I check my blood sugar and blood pressure?

If you have risk factors, check every 6 months. If you don't, get tested at least once a year after age 25. If you have diabetes or hypertension, follow your doctor's schedule.

Is it true that diabetes and BP run in families?

Yes. South Asian families — including Kashmiris — have a genetic predisposition. If your parents or grandparents have these conditions, your risk is higher.
