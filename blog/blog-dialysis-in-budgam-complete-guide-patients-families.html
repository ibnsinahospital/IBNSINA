What is Dialysis?

Dialysis is a medical procedure that performs the function of healthy kidneys when they can no longer filter waste, excess fluid, and toxins from your blood. It is used when a patient has kidney failure (end-stage renal disease) or severe kidney injury.

There are two main types:

  • Hemodialysis: Blood is filtered through a machine outside the body. Usually done 3 times a week at a hospital or dialysis center.
  • Peritoneal Dialysis: Uses the lining of your abdomen to filter blood. Can be done at home, but requires training and careful monitoring.

Most patients in Kashmir receive hemodialysis at a hospital or dialysis center. Ibn Sina Hospital provides in-house hemodialysis with modern equipment and trained staff.

What to Expect During Dialysis

If you're starting dialysis for the first time, here's a simple overview:

  • Preparation: A small surgery may be needed to create an access point (fistula or graft) for hemodialysis.
  • Session length: Each session usually takes 3–4 hours.
  • Frequency: Typically 3 times a week, but your doctor will decide based on your condition.
  • Monitoring: Your blood pressure, weight, and blood tests are regularly checked.
  • Aftercare: You can usually go home the same day and resume normal activities.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dialysis

Is dialysis painful?

Most patients do not feel pain during dialysis. You may feel a slight pinch when the needle is inserted, but the procedure itself is generally comfortable. Our staff will make sure you are as relaxed as possible.

Can I live a normal life on dialysis?

Yes, many patients on dialysis continue to work, travel, and enjoy time with family. With proper care, dialysis can help you maintain a good quality of life.

How do I know if I need dialysis?

Your nephrologist will recommend dialysis if your kidneys are functioning below 10–15% of normal. Symptoms like severe fatigue, swelling, nausea, and confusion may also indicate the need for dialysis.
