  • Genetic predisposition in South Asian populations
  • Delayed diagnosis

10 Early Warning Signs of Diabetes

1. Excessive Thirst (Polydipsia)

Feeling constantly thirsty, even after drinking water? This is often one of the first signs. Your body is trying to flush out excess sugar through urine, which dehydrates you.

2. Frequent Urination (Polyuria)

If you find yourself waking up 2-3 times at night to use the bathroom, or need to go more frequently during the day, this could be a warning sign. The kidneys work harder to remove excess sugar from your blood.

3. Unexplained Weight Loss

Losing weight without changing your diet or exercise routine? Your body may be breaking down muscle and fat for energy because it can't use sugar properly.

4. Increased Hunger (Polyphagia)

Feeling hungry soon after meals? Insulin resistance prevents sugar from entering your cells, so your body thinks it is starving and triggers hunger signals.

5. Extreme Fatigue

Low energy, constant tiredness, and feeling drained even after a full night's sleep. Without proper sugar absorption, your muscles and brain don't get enough fuel.

6. Blurred Vision

High blood sugar levels can pull fluid from the lenses of your eyes, making it harder to focus. If your vision is suddenly blurry, get it checked quickly.

7. Slow-Healing Wounds

Cuts, bruises, or sores that take weeks to heal. Diabetes affects your circulation and immune system, making recovery slower.

8. Tingling or Numbness (Neuropathy)

A "pins and needles" feeling in your hands or feet. High blood sugar damages nerves over time, affecting sensation in your extremities.

9. Frequent Infections

  • Urinary tract infections
  • Skin infections
  • Yeast infections

High blood sugar weakens your immune system and encourages bacterial and fungal growth.

10. Dark Patches on Skin (Acanthosis Nigricans)

Dark, velvety patches of skin usually on your neck, armpits, or groin. This is a sign of insulin resistance and often appears before diabetes is diagnosed.

Types of Diabetes in Kashmir

Type 1 Diabetes

  • Usually diagnosed in children and young adults
  • Body does not produce insulin
  • Requires daily insulin injections
  • Less common but still present in Kashmir

Type 2 Diabetes (Most Common in J&K)

  • Usually develops in adults over 35
  • Body produces insulin but doesn't use it well
  • Linked to lifestyle, diet, and family history
  • Can often be managed with diet, exercise, and oral medications

Gestational Diabetes

  • Develops during pregnancy
  • Usually resolves after delivery
  • Increases risk of Type 2 diabetes later

Prediabetes

  • Blood sugar is higher than normal but not high enough for diabetes
  • Reversible with lifestyle changes
  • Often goes undetected

Who Is at Higher Risk?

  • Family history – If your parents or siblings have diabetes, you are at higher risk.
  • Age – Risk increases after 35, especially for Type 2.
  • Weight – Being overweight, especially around your abdomen.
  • Diet – High consumption of rice, bread, sweets, and sugary drinks.
  • Sedentary lifestyle – Lack of physical activity.
  • Gestational diabetes – Had diabetes during pregnancy.
  • PCOS – Polycystic ovary syndrome increases risk in women.

What Kashmiri Families Should Do

1. Reduce High-Carb Foods

  • White rice → Choose brown rice, millet instead
  • Bakery items → Choose whole wheat bread instead
  • Sugary drinks → Choose water, green tea instead
  • Sweets → Choose fruits in moderation instead

2. Stay Active

  • 🚶 Walk for 30 minutes daily
  • 🏃 Gentle exercise in the morning
  • 🧘 Yoga and stretching
  • 🏋️ Light strength training

3. Monitor Your Health

  • 📋 Get a blood sugar test every 6 months
  • 📏 Track your weight
  • 🩺 Regular health checkups at a hospital

4. Get Support

  • 🩺 Consult a doctor at a trusted hospital in J&K
  • 👨👩👧 Talk to family about diet changes
  • 📱 Use health apps to track your blood sugar
