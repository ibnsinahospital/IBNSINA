What We Treat

  • Cataracts (clouding of the eye's natural lens, common with ageing)
  • Glaucoma (raised eye pressure that can damage the optic nerve)
  • Refractive errors – short-sightedness, long-sightedness, and astigmatism
  • Diabetic retinopathy (eye damage caused by diabetes)
  • Conjunctivitis (pink eye) and other eye infections
  • Dry eye syndrome and chronic eye irritation
  • Age-related macular degeneration (loss of central vision)
  • Pediatric eye problems, including squint (strabismus) and lazy eye (amblyopia)

What to Expect

1. Consultation

Your journey begins with a detailed, unhurried consultation with Dr. Rhimsha. We listen carefully to your symptoms – whether it is blurred vision, eye pain, redness, floaters, or gradual loss of sight. We also review your medical history, including any underlying conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure that can affect your eyes.

2. Diagnosis

We use advanced diagnostic equipment available right here at Ibn Sina Hospital. This includes slit-lamp examination, fundus (retinal) imaging, tonometry for glaucoma screening, and automated perimetry (visual field testing). For patients with diabetes, we perform detailed retinal assessments to detect early signs of diabetic retinopathy.

3. Treatment

Your treatment is tailored to your specific condition. It may include prescription glasses or contact lenses, medicated eye drops, laser therapy, or surgical procedures such as phacoemulsification (modern cataract surgery with lens implantation). All surgeries are performed in our state-of-the-art operation theatre with strict sterility protocols.

4. Follow-up

Recovery and long-term eye health are our priorities. We schedule regular follow-up appointments to check your healing, monitor eye pressure, update your prescription, or adjust treatment plans. We are always available to address any post-operative concerns or new symptoms.

Facilities

  • Slit-lamp biomicroscope for detailed anterior and posterior segment examination
  • Fundus camera and indirect ophthalmoscope for retinal and optic nerve imaging
  • Automated perimetry (visual field analyser) for glaucoma diagnosis and monitoring
  • Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) for high-resolution retinal and macular scans
  • Phacoemulsification machine for modern, stitch-less cataract surgery
  • Dedicated minor procedure room for foreign body removal, punctal plugs, and minor lid surgeries
  • 24/7 emergency eye care for trauma, chemical injuries, and acute angle-closure glaucoma

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I book an eye check-up at Ibn Sina Hospital?

You can book an appointment by calling our hospital reception, visiting us in person, or using our online appointment system. Our staff will help you schedule a convenient time with Dr. Rhimsha. No referral is needed for a routine eye examination.

Do you offer cataract surgery? What kind of lenses do you use?

Yes. We offer modern phacoemulsification (stitch-less) cataract surgery with intraocular lens (IOL) implantation. We use high-quality, foldable lenses that provide clear vision. Our team will help you choose the right lens based on your lifestyle and visual needs.

Do you treat eye problems related to diabetes?

Absolutely. Diabetic retinopathy is one of our key focus areas. We perform detailed retinal screening for all diabetic patients and offer laser therapy, anti-VEGF injections, and surgical management when needed – all in close coordination with our internal medicine department.

Does health insurance cover eye surgeries and treatments?

Yes. We accept most major health insurance policies and offer cashless treatment for eligible patients. Cataract surgery, glaucoma procedures, and retinal treatments are generally covered. Our billing team will guide you through the entire process. Please bring your insurance card and valid ID at the time of admission.

What should I do if I have an eye emergency – like a chemical splash, foreign object, or sudden vision loss – after hours?

Ibn Sina Hospital has a 24/7 emergency department with an ophthalmologist on call at all times. If you experience an eye injury, chemical exposure, sudden blurring, or loss of vision, do not rub your eye – come directly to the hospital or call our emergency helpline immediately. Prompt care can save your sight.
